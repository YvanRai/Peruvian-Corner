Sea & Land

6 dishes
Ceviche
Famous Japanese chefs travel to Peru to train in cooking fresh fish. Ceviche is fresh fish cubed to order, never frozen, marinated in freshly squeezed lime juice. Peru does NOT add tomatoes. Chef Miguel uses fresh local fish: mahi, papio, maguro, beef sweetbreads, salmon, red snapper and hamachi. He also has a secret local fish he won't reveal.
Signature Fresh fish
Tiradito
Peru invented Ceviche. Japanese immigrants settled along the coast and that's how Tiradito was born. Fresh fish sliced into thin sheets, dressed in lime and ají. Chef Miguel suggests extensive variations: truffled, nikkei, with coconut milk, with salmon roe.
Nikkei Variations available
Anticuchos
Street food that originated in Peru. Grilled skewers. Everyone orders the salmon or local fish version. It's a great method for cooking fish. Order a tray of fish anticuchos for catering! If you book Chef Miguel, he'll cook the beef heart version on site, so tender you'll think it's the best steak you've ever had.
Grilled
Jalea
We can't decide which is better, you decide. A mix of seafood fried in a light batter, or a mix of seafood fried and then steamed in bouillabaisse. Ask for extra octopus — Chef Miguel handles octopus like a master.
Seafood Fried
Pulpo al Olivo
Cooked octopus cut sashimi style, with a kalamata olive dressing. Chef Miguel has mastered the art of tenderizing octopus.
Signature From the Sea
Fuego de Conchitas
Scallops in their shell. These come flaming. It also works wonderfully with a mix of seafood cut pupu style.
Flambéed Seafood

Main Dishes

6 dishes
Lomo Saltado
If you made it this far, go google what Lomo Saltado is. Peru's official national dish, on the national registry. This dish was invented by the Chinese in Peru. The quintessential Peruvian stir-fry.
National Dish Chifa
Ají de Gallina
The Peruvian béchamel, with a hint of sweet and spicy, over shredded chicken with sofrito. Ají de Mariscos is the seafood version. Or try the Ají de Tofu, a take on mapo tofu. Excellent for catering or as a dip for chips, bread or celery.
Favorite
Arroz con Mariscos
Call it paella, sticky seafood rice, or seafood risotto. Why go to Spain, China or Italy when you can have all 3 in one dish? Chef Miguel makes it to order and it holds up well as a catering tray.
Seafood
Arroz con Pollo
A dish that's better cooked at home. Have Chef Miguel make a big pot for your event. He doesn't cut corners — the chicken cooks together with the rice over a manually tended flame. Golden, bone-in thighs, tender. The chicken cooks in rice with a cilantro-spinach sauce.
Traditional
Plancha
Chef Miguel spent 10 years in Argentina. This is his take on the Argentine steak. Just as wonderful with fish, seafood or soft tofu.
Grilled Argentina
Chaufas
From Chinese immigrants in Peru, chaufa is the phonetic spelling of the Chinese chao fan, or fried rice. The favorite is with salmon, and of course it can be made with any meat or tofu.
Chifa The favorite

Causa
Causa means "for the cause." Lima, 1821 — that's what locals called Peru's Independence Day from Spain. Causa was sold on the sidewalks of Lima by rebels disguised as food carts, in exchange for a slip of paper with intelligence data. It's the Peruvian take on potato salad.
History Starter
Palta Rellena
Stuffed avocado salad. It can easily pass as the vegan version of deviled eggs.
Vegan option Starter
Papa a la Huancaína
Slices of boiled potato served as potato salad with a thick dressing of fresh cheese, cream and ají. This creamy dressing can be bottled and kept as a salad dressing — it goes with everything.
Traditional Iconic dressing
Chilcano
The bony parts of the fish become an instant clear broth to open the appetite or cure a hangover. Always with a pinch of onions and lime juice.
Broth Starter
